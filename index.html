What is GRC?
The Complete Big Picture
Before touching a single screen, you must understand WHY GRC exists and what problem it solves.
🏢 Our Project: You work at TechCorp Inc. — a 500-person IT services company. Your CEO just said: "We're getting audited next quarter, we have no idea what our risks are, our policies are in random Word documents, and our vendors can access our servers without any oversight. Fix this." — That's exactly what ServiceNow GRC is for.
👨‍💼
Expert Explains

Think of GRC like the nervous system of a company's risk health. Without it, every department does its own thing — IT has a risk spreadsheet, Legal has policies in emails, Finance doesn't know what the auditors need. ServiceNow GRC puts everything in ONE place, connects them together, and makes them talk to each other automatically.

🤔 What does G, R, C actually mean?

🏛️ G — Governance

The rules of HOW your company makes decisions. Who approves what? Who is responsible for what? What are the company's policies?

Example at TechCorp: "Only the CISO can approve external system access."

⚠️ R — Risk

What BAD THINGS could happen to your company? How likely are they? How bad would they be? What do we do to prevent them?

Example at TechCorp: "Risk: A vendor could leak customer data. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High."

✅ C — Compliance

Are we following the RULES we're supposed to follow? Laws, regulations, internal policies, industry standards (SOC2, ISO 27001, GDPR).

Example at TechCorp: "We must prove to auditors we follow SOC2 controls."

Risk Framework for TechCorp
Before creating risks, we set up the scoring framework — how do we measure how bad a risk is?
📋 Scenario: TechCorp's Risk Manager says "I need a 5x5 risk matrix. Risks scored 1-5 on Likelihood and Impact. Anything scoring 15+ is Critical. We have 3 entities: IT Department, Finance Department, and Customer Portal system."
👨‍💼
Expert Explains

A Risk Framework is like a ruler for measuring risk. Without a consistent ruler, everyone measures risks differently — one person says "this is huge!" and another says "it's minor." The framework standardizes everything. In ServiceNow, this means setting up Likelihood scores, Impact scores, and the formula that combines them into a Risk Score.

🏗️ Concept: What is an Entity?

An Entity is the THING that carries the risk. It can be a department, a business unit, a technology system, a location, or a process. Every risk in GRC must belong to an Entity. Think of it as: "This risk BELONGS TO this part of the business."

For TechCorp, we'll create 3 entities: IT Department, Finance Department, and Customer Portal (a system).

Step 2: Understand the Risk Scoring Model

ServiceNow uses a matrix-based scoring system. For TechCorp, we'll use: Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact

Both Likelihood and Impact are scored 1-5. The resulting score (1-25) determines the risk rating level.

Score RangeRisk RatingColorAction Required
1 – 4Low🟢 GreenMonitor — no immediate action
5 – 9Medium🟡 YellowRisk owner must create mitigation plan within 90 days
10 – 14High🟠 OrangeEscalate to senior management — 30-day remediation
15 – 25Critical🔴 RedImmediate executive attention required — 7-day response
Step 3: The 3 Stages of Risk Assessment

Every risk goes through 3 assessment stages. Understanding these stages is critical for the exam AND for real-world GRC implementation:

1️⃣ Inherent Assessment

What is the raw risk WITHOUT any controls? If there were no MFA, no firewalls, no security team — how bad would this risk be?

TechCorp Data Breach: Likelihood 4 × Impact 5 = Inherent Score: 20 (Critical)

2️⃣ Control Assessment

How effective are our controls at reducing this risk? We assess our MFA control, firewall, and security monitoring.

Control Effectiveness: High (80%) — our controls are well-designed and operating properly.

3️⃣ Residual Assessment

What risk remains AFTER our controls? ServiceNow calculates this automatically.

Residual Score: 20 × (1-0.8) = 4 (Low) — controls reduce the risk significantly.

⚠️

Exam Tip — Target Assessment: There's also a 4th optional stage: Target Assessment — this is the risk level your company WANTS to reach in the future. It's a goal, not a measurement. If Residual = 4 (Low) but your target is 2 (Very Low), you need more controls.
